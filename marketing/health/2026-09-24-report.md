## Top 3 Marketing Highlights This Week

### 1. LLM Complexity Router — Zero-Inference Tier Selection (Strongest Current Story)

The Sep 15 community posts covered the heuristic complexity scorer in `services/llm/complexity.py` (196 lines). It runs synchronously before every LLM call with zero extra inference overhead, producing a 0–780 score across five dimensions: message length (0–150 pts), classified intent (0–300 pts), active learning scene (0–100 pts), conversation depth (0–80 pts), and complexity-marker regex (0–100 pts, capped). The markers support 14 English patterns ("prove", "derive", "step-by-step", "optimize") and 4 Chinese patterns (证明, 比较, etc.) for bilingual sessions. Scores below 150 → fast tier, 150–499 → standard, 500+ → frontier. A preference change ("switch to dark mode") scores ~50 and never touches the frontier model; a multi-step proof in an exam-prep scene hits 500+. **This prevents the classic failure mode of routing every request to the most expensive model.**

This story has been queued at the top for two weeks. If the Sep 15 HN post wasn't submitted yet, this is the week — the lead ("How we stopped sending 'ok thanks' to our frontier model") is platform-ready. **Action: Submit the Sep 15 Show HN draft.** Target: Hacker News, r/LocalLLaMA, r/MachineLearning.

### 2. Circuit Breaker + Provider Fallback Chain — Reference Implementation for Local LLM Reliability

The Sep 15 posts also covered `services/llm/circuit_breaker.py` (83 lines): `CircuitBreakerMixin` tracks health per provider with progressive cooldown steps `[5, 10, 20, 60]` seconds, opens after 3 consecutive failures, resets after 120 seconds. The `ProviderRegistry` walks a typed fallback chain (model variant hints → primary → ordered fallback) with a background health probe loop every 30 seconds. When Ollama OOM-kills itself mid-session, the app falls back to a cloud provider without the user noticing. **Most local LLM projects have no fallback strategy; this is a complete, MIT-licensed reference implementation.** Target: r/LocalLLaMA, r/selfhosted, IndieHackers.

### 3. LOOM + LECTOR: The Strongest Technical Differentiator Still Needs a Dedicated Post

LOOM (`loom_graph.py`, 428 lines) builds a per-student knowledge graph with FSRS-decayed mastery scores. LECTOR (`lector.py`, 344 lines) reorders the review queue: prerequisites before dependents even if dependent cards are more overdue, confusion pairs co-reviewed for contrast, proactive interference detection before the mistake rather than after. **No other Anki-compatible or FSRS-based tool does this.** This system has been mentioned in community posts but hasn't received a dedicated Show HN or r/Anki post that puts it center stage. A standalone post titled "Show HN: Knowledge-graph-aware spaced repetition — prerequisites before dependents" would differentiate OpenTutor from every other AI flashcard tool. Target: Hacker News, r/Anki, r/medicalschool, r/MCAT.
