## Top 3 Marketing Highlights This Week

### 1. FSRS-5/6 Upgrade — Trainable Forgetting Curve
The Sep 1 community post explains the upgrade from FSRS-4.5 (17 parameters) to FSRS-5/6 (21 parameters). Key additions: a trainable `decay` parameter so the forgetting curve `R(t) = (1 + factor·t/S)^(-decay)` can be optimized per user rather than fixed; separate same-day review stability handling to prevent intra-day reviews from inflating stability; exponential difficulty initialization that widens the gap between "Again" and "Easy" first reviews. This differentiates OpenTutor from 99% of AI tutors that ship fixed-parameter SM-2. **Target communities: r/Anki, r/MCAT, r/medicalschool.**

### 2. Cognitive Load Auto-Tuner — 136 Lines, No ML
Week 35 build-in-public post covers `cognitive_load_tuning.py`: a per-user 20-message sliding window that dampens signals with rolling mean ≥ 0.65 (multiplier decays ×0.90 per update, floor 0.40; recovers at ×1.04). Prevents signal saturation where high-baseline students permanently saturate the load score. This is the kind of specific, technically honest engineering detail that performs well on Hacker News and r/LocalLLaMA. **Best hook:** "My cognitive load detector was permanently alarmed. 136 lines to make it useful again."

### 3. LLM Circuit Breaker + Three-Tier Complexity Router
The Sep 1 community posts detail `CircuitBreakerMixin` (83 lines): progressive cooldown steps `[5, 10, 20, 60]s`, circuit opens after 3 consecutive failures, 120s auto-reset. Layered above: a complexity router that routes to fast/standard/frontier tiers using 5 heuristics — no inference call required. 40% of typical session requests route to the fast tier (llama3.2:3b); average latency drops ~30% versus no routing. Concrete numbers + code snippets = high-signal content for local LLM builders. **Target: r/LocalLLaMA, indie hackers building on top of Ollama.**
