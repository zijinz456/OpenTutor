## Top 3 Marketing Highlights This Week

### 1. Show HN: Real-time cognitive load detection from 12 behavioral signals
The Oct 6 community post batch includes a polished Show HN submission on OpenTutor's cognitive load scorer (`apps/api/services/cognitive_load.py`, 1,110 lines across 4 modules). The pitch is technically precise and HN-appropriate: it leads with the behavioral-signal-as-structured-value design decision rather than feature marketing. The 12 signals are enumerated with explicit weights (summing to 1.0), the NLP affect caching strategy is explained, and the per-user relative-baseline calibrator is called out. **This is ready to post.** Key hook: *"The obvious fix — 'detect if the user seems confused' — is slippery when you put it in a prompt, because it relies on the LLM to infer something the data already tells you directly."*

### 2. Socratic FSM Twitter thread — week 40 build-in-public content
The Week 40 post (Oct 4, `f14968a`) covers the 5-state Socratic questioning engine: PROBE, CLARIFY, CONFRONT, SCAFFOLD, CONFIRM with 20 explicit state transitions. The Twitter thread is 6 tweets and technically strong — it includes the initial-state selection logic (cognitive load threshold → SCAFFOLD, mastery threshold → PROBE), the escape hatch after 4 consecutive SCAFFOLD turns, and an honest question about whether 5 states is the right granularity. **This is the most shareable technical content in weeks.** Strong for the educational technology and AI builder audience.

### 3. r/LocalLLaMA: tiered LLM calls pattern
The Oct 6 community post batch includes an r/LocalLLaMA post on OpenTutor's two-call pattern per turn: a small/fast classifier call (single-word output: correct/partial/wrong/confused/no_response) followed by a large generation call with a specific state-based directive. The pattern generalizes beyond OpenTutor — anyone building multi-step agents on local hardware with a mix of model sizes benefits from this. The `.env` config example (`LLM_MODEL_FAST=llama3.2:3b`) is concrete and copy-pasteable. **Right audience, right technical depth.**
