## Top 3 Marketing Highlights This Week

### 1. LOOM + LECTOR: Prerequisite-Aware Review Ordering with FSRS-Decayed Knowledge Graph Mastery

The Sep 8 community posts detail the most architecturally distinctive feature in OpenTutor: LOOM (`loom_graph.py`, 428 lines) builds a per-student knowledge graph with concepts typed as `prerequisite`, `related`, and `confused_with`. Mastery scores are continuously decayed using FSRS retrievability — `mastery = raw_mastery * R(elapsed, stability)` — so a concept you understood three weeks ago but haven't touched since will correctly surface as weak, not just overdue for a card review. LECTOR (`lector.py`, 344 lines) then reorders the review queue based on this graph: prerequisites surface before dependents (even if the dependent's cards are more overdue), confusion pairs are co-reviewed for contrast, and proactive interference detection fires before you make the mistake rather than after. **No other Anki-compatible tool does this.** Target communities: r/Anki, r/medicalschool, r/MCAT.

### 2. Cognitive Load Detector — 12 Behavioral Signals, No Inference Call

The Sep 8 Hacker News Show HN draft covers `services/cognitive_load.py` (488 lines): a real-time 0.0–1.0 load score computed on every incoming message from 12 signals — fatigue markers, session length (CLT-backed 45-min degradation model), recent error rate, message brevity, help-seeking keywords, quiz accuracy, hesitation timing, NLP affect (frustration + confusion weighted separately, cached every 3 messages to avoid latency), per-user relative baseline calibration, wrong-answer streaks, message gap timing, and repeated errors on the same problem. The score injects into the tutor's system prompt mid-session. The decay counter (sustained high-load decrements by 1 on a non-high message rather than resetting) prevents a single correct answer from wiping the history of sustained struggle. **Best hook:** "My AI tutor kept pushing harder content at overwhelmed students. 12 signals and 488 lines to make it stop." Target: Hacker News, r/LocalLLaMA.

### 3. Week-Over-Week Dashboard Deltas — The "Am I Improving?" Problem

The Week 36 build-in-public post covers the new `WeeklyStatsSection`: a `WeeklyReport` API shape with `this_week`, `last_week`, and `deltas` objects across three metrics — `study_minutes`, `accuracy`, and `active_days`. The frontend renders them explicitly side by side (this week large, last week small, delta in green/red) rather than as trend lines. The three dimensions are intentionally independent: high accuracy + low time = reviewing easy material; low accuracy + high time = grinding hard material; many active days + short sessions = scattered engagement. The Week 36 post also ships the 12th and final composable learning block — `summary-block.tsx` (118 lines) — completing the block system introduced at launch. **Milestone framing:** "The 12th block is done. The workspace is complete." Target: IndieHackers, Twitter/X dev community.
