## Top 3 Marketing Highlights This Week

### 1. LLM Complexity Router — Zero-Inference Tier Selection Across Fast/Standard/Frontier

The Sep 15 community posts detail the heuristic complexity scorer in `services/llm/complexity.py` (196 lines). It runs synchronously before every LLM call with no extra inference overhead and produces a 0–780 score across five dimensions: message length (0–150 pts), classified intent (0–300 pts), active learning scene (0–100 pts), conversation depth (0–80 pts), and complexity-marker regex (0–100 pts, capped). The markers include 14 English patterns ("prove", "derive", "step-by-step", "optimize") and 4 Chinese patterns (证明, 比较, etc.) for bilingual sessions. Scores below 150 go to the fast tier, 150–499 to standard, 500+ to frontier. A preference change ("switch to dark mode") scores ~50 and never touches the frontier model; a multi-step proof in an exam-prep scene hits 500+. This prevents the classic failure mode of routing every request — including "ok thanks" — to the most expensive model. **Best hook for HN:** "How we stopped sending 'ok thanks' to our frontier model." Target: Hacker News, r/LocalLLaMA, r/MachineLearning.

### 2. Circuit Breaker + Provider Fallback Chain — Production Reliability for Local LLM Apps

The same Sep 15 posts cover `services/llm/circuit_breaker.py` (83 lines): a `CircuitBreakerMixin` any LLM client subclasses, tracking health per provider with progressive cooldown steps `[5, 10, 20, 60]` seconds, auto-opens after 3 consecutive failures, and auto-resets after 120 seconds. The `ProviderRegistry` walks a typed fallback chain (model variant hints → primary → ordered fallback) and runs a background health probe loop every 30 seconds to keep latency-annotated probe data fresh. When Ollama OOM-kills itself mid-session, the app falls back to a cloud provider without the user noticing. **Best hook:** "When your local model OOM-kills itself mid-tutoring-session." Most local LLM projects have no fallback strategy; this is a complete, MIT-licensed reference implementation. Target: r/LocalLLaMA, r/selfhosted, IndieHackers.

### 3. LOOM + LECTOR: FSRS-Decayed Knowledge Graph Mastery (Still the Strongest Technical Story)

The Sep 8 posts laid out the most architecturally distinctive feature in the project: LOOM (`loom_graph.py`, 428 lines) builds a per-student knowledge graph decaying mastery scores via FSRS retrievability — `mastery = raw_mastery × R(elapsed, stability)` — so a concept understood three weeks ago but untouched since correctly surfaces as weak. LECTOR (`lector.py`, 344 lines) reorders the review queue based on this graph: prerequisites before dependents even if dependent cards are more overdue, confusion pairs co-reviewed for contrast, proactive interference detection before the mistake rather than after. **No other Anki-compatible or FSRS-based tool does this.** The Sep 15 posts link all three systems (FSRS decay + LOOM graph + complexity router) for the first time. Target: r/Anki, r/medicalschool, r/MCAT, Hacker News.
